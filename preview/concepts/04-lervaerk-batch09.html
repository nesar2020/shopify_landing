Process

Three glazes, one firing, no second chances.

Reduction firing means starving the kiln of oxygen at 1280 °C. It's what pulls the green out of iron and the depth out of ash — and it's why no two pieces, even side by side in the kiln, come out alike.

Week 1–2

Thrown & turned

All 24 forms thrown from one 80 kg lot of Bornholm stoneware, then trimmed and stamped while leather-hard.

Week 3 · 950 °C

Bisque firing

A slow first firing opens the clay to accept glaze. Pieces that crack here never reach the catalogue.

Week 4 · 1280 °C

Reduction firing — 14 hours

The kiln is fired overnight and read by eye and cone, not just controller. This is where the batch becomes itself.

Week 5

Graded & numbered

Each survivor is water-tested, graded and entered into the batch record. Seconds are broken, not discounted.

Practical notes

Before you reserve.

Is it dishwasher and food safe?

Yes — all three Batch 09 glazes are food-safe and vitrified at 1280 °C. Dishwasher tolerated, hand-wash recommended; the glaze will outlive either of us regardless.

What does "the glaze is retired" mean?

The Ask recipe — this exact wood-ash blend — will not be mixed again after Batch 09. Future batches get new recipes. It keeps every batch a body of work, not a product line.

How does shipping work for something this fragile?

Double-boxed in moulded pulp (no plastic), fully insured, shipped within the EU in 2–4 days. A piece that arrives damaged is refunded in full — photos are enough, don't ship it back.

Can I see pieces in person?

The studio in Copenhagen NV opens the first Saturday of each month. Batch 10 will be in the kiln — come see what a firing looks like mid-story.
